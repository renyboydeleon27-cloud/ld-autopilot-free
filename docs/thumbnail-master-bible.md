Approved production behavior: generate the portrait thumbnail **visibly zoomed out / pulled back at native size** so a later centered Zoom/Fill in InShot can tighten the composition without losing essential detail.

Canonical working canvas: **1080 × 1920 (9:16)**.

- strong full-color historical anime / graphic-novel treatment;
- dynamic disaster-family headline;
- dynamic yellow-strip hook;
- concise event/place/year line;
- slightly wider native framing for InShot;

This is now the **FINAL Living Disaster Book thumbnail reference** unless a future explicit master revision replaces it.

## Core purpose
Every Living Disaster Book portrait thumbnail must preserve post-editing headroom. The native generation should not already consume the final crop.

**Production rule: generate wide first, crop later.**

This applies to **ALL 10 Thumbnail Design variants**. Design-specific composition may vary, but no design may push essential headline text, event line, faces, hands, trigger objects, or hazard cues into unsafe outer edges.

## Universal strong InShot-safe geometry
- Canvas: **1080 × 1920**.
- Unified essential composition scale: roughly **74–78%** of the full canvas.
- Essential safe frame: **x 12–88%** and **y 15–85%**.
- Target robustness: preserve essential content after roughly **15–18% centered InShot Zoom/Fill**.
- Outer frame is nonessential scene bleed only.
- Outer bleed may contain sky, smoke, haze, distant terrain, architecture continuation, rubble, ground, water, vegetation, dust, clothing, debris, or other event-appropriate scene material.

## Master priority
If a selected Thumbnail Design conflicts with this geometry or creates an already-tight final crop, **the Universal Strong InShot Edit-Safe Master Format wins**.

The 10-design system may change subject side, camera height, posture, hazard scale, object emphasis, symbol, or environmental focus. It may not remove editor headroom.

## Camera-distance lock
Use a visibly wider / more pulled-back camera framing than a normal final poster.

The native thumbnail must:
- preserve visible crop allowance on all sides;
- keep critical elements inward from frame edges;
- avoid enlarging a person, trigger object, hazard, or symbol simply to fill empty space;
- remain visually complete before editing;
- become tighter and stronger after moderate centered zoom.

## Title protected box
Keep the entire title system inside **x 12–88%**, **y 15–30%**.

- Main headline: **y 15–21.5%**.
- Yellow hook strip: **y 21.5–25.5%**.
- Event/location/year line: **y 25.5–30%**.
- Top **0–15%** is nonessential scene/sky/smoke breathing room only.
- No headline pixel may enter the top bleed region.
- Long headlines should reduce size or use a compact two-line layout inside the protected title box instead of cropping.

## Dynamic topic text system
Do **not** default every episode to `DISASTER / WHEN DISASTER STRUCK`.

Use:
1. **BIG WHITE HEADLINE** = event/disaster family appropriate to the current topic.
2. **YELLOW STRIP** = short family/topic-specific hook.
3. **EVENT ID LINE** = concise place/country/region/year/date derived only from the supplied current topic.

Examples include:
- CHEMICAL ATTACK — WHEN CHEMICAL WEAPONS STRUCK
- AVALANCHE — WHEN THE SNOW BROKE LOOSE
- TORNADO — WHEN THE WIND STRUCK
- TSUNAMI — WHEN THE SEA RUSHED IN
- CYCLONE — WHEN THE STORM HIT
- LOCUST PLAGUE — WHEN THE SWARM ARRIVED

Never invent a location, date, statistic, record claim, or casualty figure.

## Human-subject designs
For normal survivor/responder/escape/rescue thumbnails:
- Dominant adult width should usually remain around **26–34% of canvas width**.
- Keep full head, face, torso and required hands inside the protected scene area.
- Pull the subject inward and slightly upward.
- Never enlarge the person merely to fill empty space.
- Do not let the subject cover the only recognizable disaster cue.

Extreme-close-up / eye designs may intentionally enlarge the intended facial crop, but critical eyes/pupils and the complete title system must still survive centered zoom.

## Trigger-object / symbol / landmark designs
- Keep the critical trigger object, symbol, landmark or hazard readable after centered zoom.
- Pull important foreground objects upward/inward from crop boundaries.
- The only recognizable event cue may not live solely in the outer bleed zone.

## Hazard readability
The disaster must remain recognizable after later editor zoom.

- Keep critical hazard cues inside the essential composition.
- Preserve foreground-midground-background separation.
- Do not allow the main subject to hide the only readable hazard cue.

## FINAL bottom-area lock — NO FOOTER
The lower area is **visual scene only**.

Do not render:
- `LIVING DISASTER BOOK`
- book/globe icon
- channel logo
- `REAL DISASTERS. REAL HISTORY. STORIES WE SHOULD NEVER FORGET.`
- any footer branding
- any bottom tagline
- any lower caption
- any decorative badge added only to fill space

From roughly **y 74–100%**, continue the actual illustration naturally: ground, rubble, road, water, snow, vegetation, clothing, debris, atmosphere, or other event-appropriate scene material.

The bottom must feel clean, uncluttered, and cinematic.

## Whole-composition compression lock
If a draft feels tight, uniformly reduce and recenter the entire essential composition together:
- headline
- yellow hook strip
- event line
- main subject / trigger object / symbol
- hazard emphasis

Do not solve crop pressure by cutting one hand, cropping a face edge, pushing text into the top border, or enlarging the subject.
