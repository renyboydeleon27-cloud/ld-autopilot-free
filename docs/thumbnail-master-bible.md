# Living Disaster Book — Thumbnail Master Bible

Approved crop/scale reference: **San Francisco Earthquake & Fire — 1906**, Design No. 4 — Looking Back / Escape.
Reference asset: `assets/thumbnail-design-4-approved.png`.
Reference source ratio: **864 × 1536 (9:16)**. Canonical working canvas: **1080 × 1920 (9:16)**.

## Core purpose
Future thumbnails must remain readable after a moderate centered Zoom/Fill in InShot. Do not build the important content edge-to-edge. Compose the finished thumbnail as if the important poster content has been uniformly reduced to about **82–85% scale** and centered on event-specific background bleed.

## Universal portrait crop-safe geometry
- Canvas: **1080 × 1920**.
- Essential safe frame: **x 8–92%** and **y 10–90%**.
  - Approx. pixels: **x 86–994**, **y 192–1728**.
- Keep about **8–10% nonessential scene bleed** at left/right and **9–10% bleed** at top/bottom.
- No essential title letters, face, hands, event line, logo, or footer outside the essential safe frame.
- Target robustness: essential content should survive roughly **12–15% centered editor zoom**. Arbitrary heavy zoom is not considered safe.

## Title block
Keep the title system compact and high, but never touching the top edge.
- Main headline: **y 11–21%**.
- Yellow hook strip: **y 21–26%**.
- Event/location/year line: **y 26–30%**.
- Title width should normally stay within **x 9–91%**.
- Use the top 0–10% only for nonessential sky/smoke/scene bleed.

## Character scale and position — Design No. 4 Looking Back / Escape
For the approved right-side Looking Back composition:
- Overall character box: approximately **x 50–92%**, **y 32–86%**.
- Character maximum width: about **38–42% of canvas width**.
- Character should render about **20–25% smaller** than the oversized Xylazine attempts that required repeated InShot correction.
- Full head top: **y 32–35%**.
- Head center target: **x 72–76%**, **y 41–44%**.
- Face must remain completely inside the frame with breathing room above hair.
- Torso remains readable through the middle-lower region.
- **Both hands must be fully visible above y 78%** whenever the design requires visible hands/action.
- Lower body may continue below the hands, but it must not become essential to understanding the thumbnail.

## Hazard / environment zone
- Main hazard, aftermath, or response activity normally occupies the opposite side: roughly **x 5–60%**, **y 30–80%** for Design No. 4.
- Keep enough of the event environment visible that the disaster/crisis is recognizable at phone size.
- Do not let the foreground character cover the only recognizable hazard cue.

## Footer / branding
- Keep all important footer branding within **y 80–88%**.
- Keep footer content inside **x 8–92%**.
- Leave the bottom **12% or more** mostly as nonessential scene/texture bleed when practical.
- Branding is secondary to the title and subject and must not sit directly on the bottom edge.

## Compression lock
When a generated draft feels visually strong but gets cropped in InShot, the correct fix is **not** to move only one label or crop one limb. The entire essential composition must be reduced together: title block, main character, event line, and footer. Preserve their relative hierarchy while increasing outer scene bleed.

## Design flexibility
This bible controls **scale, safety margins, and crop survival**, not creative sameness. The 10-design rotation may still vary subject side, camera height, body pose, hazard placement, and visual emphasis. Each design must map its essential content into this same safe geometry unless a design-specific rule is even more conservative.

## Event accuracy
The San Francisco sample is a geometry/scale reference only. Never reuse its fire, buildings, rails, clothing, skyline, or event wording for unrelated disasters. Every future thumbnail must rebuild the hazard, architecture, clothing, props, environment, and text for the current topic.
