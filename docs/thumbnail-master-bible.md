# Living Disaster Book — FINAL Thumbnail Master Reference v7

Approved production behavior: generate each portrait thumbnail **visibly zoomed out / pulled back at native size** so a later centered Zoom/Fill in InShot can tighten the composition without losing essential detail.

Canonical working canvas: **1080 × 1920 (9:16)**.

## Final approved reference behavior
The approved reference is the latest Halabja-style composition with:
- strong full-color historical anime / graphic-novel treatment;
- dynamic disaster-family headline;
- dynamic yellow-strip hook;
- concise event/place/year line;
- slightly wider native framing for InShot;
- one dominant adult subject kept inward and fully readable;
- recognizable hazard/environment retained after zoom;
- a **clean, low-detail title backdrop** above and immediately behind the headline;
- **fresh composition rather than recycled poses/layouts**;
- event/year/location-appropriate architecture, clothing, vehicles, terrain, utilities and hazard behavior;
- **NO footer branding, NO channel logo, NO globe/book icon, NO bottom tagline, and NO lower caption text**;
- natural illustrated scene continuing cleanly to the bottom edge.

This is the **FINAL Living Disaster Book Thumbnail Reference v7** unless a future explicit master revision replaces it.

## Core production rule
**Generate wide first, crop later.**

The native thumbnail should not already consume the final crop. It must remain complete before editing and become tighter/stronger after centered InShot Zoom/Fill.

This applies to **ALL 10 Thumbnail Design variants**.

## Universal strong InShot-safe geometry
- Canvas: **1080 × 1920**.
- Unified essential composition scale: roughly **74–78%** of the full canvas.
- Essential safe frame: **x 12–88%** and **y 15–85%**.
- Target robustness: preserve essential content after roughly **15–18% centered InShot Zoom/Fill**.
- Outer frame is nonessential scene bleed only.
- Outer bleed may contain sky, smoke, haze, distant terrain, architecture continuation, rubble, ground, water, vegetation, dust, clothing, debris, or other event-appropriate scene material.

## Master priority
If a selected Thumbnail Design conflicts with this geometry or creates an already-tight crop, **the Universal Strong InShot Edit-Safe Master Format wins**.

The 10-design system may vary subject side, camera height, posture, hazard scale, foreground object, symbol, environment emphasis, or movement. It may not remove editor headroom.

## Clean title backdrop lock
The area above and immediately behind the headline must remain visually quiet enough for maximum mobile readability.

Prefer:
- open sky;
- smoke/haze;
- cloud;
- soft atmospheric color;
- low-detail event-appropriate background.

Avoid placing a large mountain ridge, landmark, roofline, tower, vehicle, face, or busy structure directly behind or above the headline when it competes with the typography.

If mountains, landmarks, towers, or other major environment features are essential to the event, keep them clearly visible **below the title block or elsewhere in the main scene** rather than crowding the headline.

## Title protected box
Keep the complete title system inside **x 12–88%**, **y 15–30%**.

- Main headline: **y 15–21.5%**.
- Yellow hook strip: **y 21.5–25.5%**.
- Event/location/year line: **y 25.5–30%**.
- Top **0–15%** is nonessential sky/smoke/environment breathing room only.
- Long headlines should shrink or use a compact two-line layout inside the protected box rather than crop.

## Dynamic topic text system
Do **not** default every episode to `DISASTER / WHEN DISASTER STRUCK`.

Use:
1. **BIG WHITE HEADLINE** = current event/disaster family.
2. **YELLOW STRIP** = short truthful family/topic-specific hook.
3. **EVENT ID LINE** = concise place/country/region/year/date derived only from the current topic.

Examples:
- CHEMICAL ATTACK — WHEN CHEMICAL WEAPONS STRUCK
- AVALANCHE — WHEN THE SNOW BROKE LOOSE
- TORNADO — WHEN THE WIND STRUCK
- TSUNAMI — WHEN THE SEA RUSHED IN
- CYCLONE — WHEN THE STORM HIT
- LOCUST PLAGUE — WHEN THE SWARM ARRIVED

Never invent a location, date, statistic, record claim, or casualty figure.

## Universal fresh-composition / anti-duplicate lock
Every thumbnail must feel newly staged for its current topic while preserving the same master format.

Do **not** clone or trace a recent thumbnail's:
- main face;
- hairstyle;
- wardrobe pattern;
- body pose;
- hand gesture;
- subject side;
- camera height;
- camera distance;
- foreground-object arrangement;
- hazard placement;
- crowd arrangement;
- background geometry.

Avoid repeatedly defaulting to the same leaning-forward survivor, same centered face, same hand-to-mouth gesture, same evacuation pose, or same left/right hazard split when another truthful composition works.

Supporting adults must not be duplicated, mirrored, or rendered as accidental twins.

If the same event is regenerated, create a **fresh composition** rather than reproducing the previous image.

The 10-design rotation should continue to vary focal strategy across projects.

## Event accuracy lock
The current topic, location/region, and supplied year/date are authoritative.

Every thumbnail must rebuild the image for that event only and match the period/place in:
- architecture and construction language;
- street or ground materials;
- terrain and vegetation;
- utilities and infrastructure;
- vehicles and transport;
- tools and equipment;
- civilian clothing and role;
- event-supported hazard behavior.

Never import disaster-specific scenery or effects from another project: no unrelated fire, waves, snow, skyline, vehicles, buildings, props, or clothing.

Do not invent a named landmark, readable sign, flag, institution, exact street plan, or modern object merely for drama.

When a detail is uncertain, use restrained **period-plausible local forms** rather than false specificity.

Historical/event accuracy outranks spectacle.

## Human-subject designs
For normal survivor/responder/escape/rescue thumbnails:
- dominant adult width should usually remain around **26–34% of canvas width**;
- keep full head, face, torso and required hands inside the protected scene area;
- pull the subject inward and slightly upward;
- never enlarge the person merely to fill empty space;
- do not let the subject hide the only recognizable disaster cue.

Extreme-close-up / eye designs may intentionally enlarge the intended facial crop, but critical eyes/pupils and the complete title system must still survive centered zoom.

## Trigger-object / symbol / landmark designs
- Keep the critical trigger object, symbol, landmark or hazard readable after centered zoom.
- Pull important foreground objects upward/inward from crop boundaries.
- The only recognizable event cue may not live solely in the outer bleed zone.
- A landmark or terrain feature must never be invented simply to make the frame look dramatic.

## Hazard readability
The disaster must remain recognizable after later editor zoom.

- Keep critical hazard cues inside the essential composition.
- Preserve foreground-midground-background separation.
- Do not allow the main subject to hide the only readable hazard cue.
- Keep physical behavior plausible for the event family.

## FINAL bottom-area lock — NO FOOTER
The lower area is **visual scene only**.

Do not render:
- `LIVING DISASTER BOOK`
- book/globe icon
- channel logo
- `REAL DISASTERS. REAL HISTORY. STORIES WE SHOULD NEVER FORGET.`
- any footer branding
- any bottom tagline
- any lower caption
- any decorative badge added only to fill space

From roughly **y 74–100%**, continue the actual illustration naturally: ground, rubble, road, water, snow, vegetation, clothing, debris, atmosphere, or other event-appropriate scene material.

The bottom must feel clean, uncluttered, and cinematic.

## Whole-composition compression lock
If a draft feels tight, uniformly reduce and recenter the entire essential composition together:
- headline
- yellow hook strip
- event line
- main subject / trigger object / symbol
- hazard emphasis

Do not solve crop pressure by cutting one hand, cropping a face edge, pushing text into the top border, or enlarging the subject.

## InShot survival test
After an imagined centered **15–18% Zoom/Fill**, these must remain visible:
- full headline
- full yellow hook strip
- full event line
- face
- required hands
- critical trigger object
- critical hazard cue

Footer survival is intentionally irrelevant because **there is no footer**.

## Approved-reference rule
Any approved sample, including Halabja, is a **format/quality reference only**.

Never copy its specific person, scarf, pose, street, mountain, mosque/minaret, toxic cloud placement, vehicle arrangement, or other event-specific visual detail into unrelated episodes.

Preserve the quality standard, not the literal image.
